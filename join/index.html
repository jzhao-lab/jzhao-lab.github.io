Zhao Lab will explore research at the intersection of translational bioelectronics, AI-driven intelligent healthcare, smart garments, robotics, embodied intelligence & interactions, and emerging micro/nano-electronics. Connect about human and technology through materials, devices, advanced manufacturing, system integrations, multimodal data analytics, and translational deployment.

We welcome applicants from Mechanical engineering, Electrical & Computer engineering, Materials science, Bioengineering, Biomedical engineering, Computer science, AI, and related disciplines.

Skills and experiences

We welcome applicants with skills and experiences including, but not limited to:

  • Bioelectronics, biomaterials, and bio-interfaces
  • Translational healthcare and women's health
  • E-textiles and soft, flexible, stretchable electronics
  • Wearable sensors, tactile and haptic interfaces
  • Robotics — exoskeletons, soft, and medical robots
  • Electronics, micro/nano fabrication, and advanced manufacturing
  • Sensing, semiconductors, and optoelectronic devices
  • Multimodal data analysis, fusion, and AI

Graduate students (MS & PhD)

Prospective MS and PhD students are encouraged to apply through the official UW–Madison program pages below and to list Dr. Zhao as a potential advisor. I also encourage you to reach out to discuss shared research interests. - When reaching out, you are encouraged to include: (i) CV with full publication lists and three references; (ii)  a brief description of research background; (iii) relevant research skills and experiences, if any (iv) research topic(s) that excite you the most (v) research topics that you dislike, if any

Postdoctoral researchers

Get in touch to discuss shared research interests and potential collaborations. A CV, a short research statement, and your anticipated timing are helpful starting points.

Undergraduates & research interns

Share your interests, relevant coursework or project experience, and anticipated availability. Tell me about something you have built, measured, or analyzed, and what you would like to explore next.

Visiting researchers

Reach out about potential research connections or visits. Include your experience, research interests, and preferred timing so we can discuss possible fit.
